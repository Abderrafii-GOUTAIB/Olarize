# AI_HR_Business_V1_Notion

# Business V1 — AI-Native HR for Morocco

## 1. Target Customer

Moroccan **private companies with approximately 20–200 employees**, especially those still using:

- Excel
- Paper
- WhatsApp
- Basic HR software
- Fragmented payroll tools

## 2. Problem

HR teams lose significant time:

- Navigating multiple systems
- Calculating leave and attendance
- Preparing payroll variables
- Managing CNSS and CIMR data
- Checking Moroccan HR rules
- Answering repetitive employee questions
- Correcting administrative and payroll errors

These inefficiencies create direct costs through wasted time, payroll errors, compliance risk, and poor employee experience.

## 3. Solution

An **AI-native Moroccan HR operating system** where HR, managers, and employees mainly interact through natural language.

### Example requests

> “Who is absent today?”
> 

> “How much will Ahmed cost the company this month?”
> 

> “Prepare September payroll variables.”
> 

> “How much CNSS and CIMR are due?”
> 

> “Which contracts expire next month?”
> 

AI is not an added chatbot. It is the **primary way users interact with the HR system**.

Traditional dashboards, tables, and forms appear mainly when visual review, approval, or detailed data management is useful.

## 4. V1 Business Scope

### Included

- Employee records
- Employment contracts
- Attendance
- Leave and absence management
- HR documents
- Payroll preparation
- CNSS
- CIMR
- Employee self-service
- AI interface
- Moroccan private-sector HR rules

### Excluded from V1

- Public-sector HR
- Recruitment
- Performance management
- Training management
- Automated disciplinary decisions
- Autonomous dismissal or sensitive legal decisions
- Full proprietary gross-to-net payroll engine

## 5. Positioning

### We will not position it as:

> “Another Moroccan HR software with AI.”
> 

### Position it as:

> **“The AI operating system for HR in Morocco.”**
> 

The differentiation is the operating model:

**Ask → Analyze → Calculate → Prepare → Confirm → Execute**

instead of forcing users to navigate traditional HR software menus.

## 6. Business Model

Recurring GaaS subscription.

### Initial pricing hypothesis

| Plan | Indicative Price |
| --- | --- |
| Starter | ~1,500 MAD / month |
| Growth | ~2,500–3,000 MAD / month |
| Larger companies | Custom pricing |

### Future monetization options

- Per-employee pricing
- Premium payroll/compliance modules
- Advanced AI functionality
- ERP/payroll integrations
- Enterprise plans
- Multi-entity management

## 7. Core Value Proposition

The product should sell measurable business outcomes:

**Less HR administration + fewer payroll errors + faster answers + stronger compliance + lower operating cost**

### Example sales logic

> “Your HR team currently spends 60 hours per month on repetitive administrative work. Our system materially reduces that workload for 2,500 MAD per month.”
> 

The product should demonstrate that the **financial value created is significantly higher than the subscription cost**.

## 8. Initial Validation Target

Before serious fundraising:

1. Interview **10–15 HR managers**
2. Secure **3–5 pilot companies**
3. Build the working MVP
4. Convert at least **3 companies into paying customers**
5. Measure :
    - Time saved
    - Usage frequency
    - AI accuracy
    - Tasks automated
    - Error reduction
    - Customer retention
    - AI/infrastructure cost per company
6. Document unit economics

## 9. V1 Vision

**Moroccan private sector first.**

**20–200 employee companies first.**

**AI-first, not chatbot-added.**

**CNSS + CIMR included.**

**Payroll preparation first; full payroll engine later.**

**Public sector later as a separate rules layer.**

## 10. Strategic Objective

Build a focused Moroccan HR product that proves:

- Strong recurring usage
- Clear ROI
- High retention
- Reliable AI execution
- Morocco-specific compliance knowledge
- Scalable SaaS economics

Once validated in the Moroccan private sector, expand into:

1. Larger Moroccan companies
2. Additional HR/payroll modules
3. Public-sector rules
4. Other Francophone African markets
